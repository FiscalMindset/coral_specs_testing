Why v2 reaches more passes than v1▶
  • v1 strategy: long-lived keychain OAuth refresh token — ran for 47m without re-auth but couldn't recover from timeouts (only did ad-hoc /tmp/retimeout.py probes for 5 tables).
  • v2 strategy: short-lived az-minted admin token pasted via env var — re-authed twice mid-run when the token expired; after the main run, ran an 88-table retry sweep covering all 51 expired-token and 37 timeout tables.
  • Result: 13 of the 88 retried tables returned PASS on the second attempt (slow endpoints). The 5 net gain in pass count (122 → 127) is exactly: 13 retries that became PASS, minus 8 retries that flipped to other categories (auth/wrong_audience/etc.).
  • Trade-off: v2 takes ~2h 28m vs v1's 47m. The extra time is the 2 re-auths + 88-table retry sweep. The pass-rate gain is small (5 tables) but the data is more complete (no expired-token failures left in the final set).
